## Step 11 — Capacity-aware account routing

Route worker/QA dispatch toward accounts with the most combined headroom — daily budget and
concurrent capacity together, both normalized so accounts on different tiers compare fairly —
not just the fewest currently-active sessions.

CONTEXT
AccountPool.acquire() currently sorts eligible accounts by active_sessions ascending only:

    eligible.sort(key=lambda a: a.active_sessions)

This spreads concurrent load well, but ignores daily_tasks_used entirely. Two accounts on the
same plan tier with the same active_sessions count get treated identically even if one is at
2 of its 15 daily tasks and the other is at 14 of 15 — the second will hit
AccountPoolExhausted for the rest of the day well before the first does.

REQUIREMENTS
- Sort by a combined score, not a strict lexicographic ordering: pure "budget first, sessions
  only as an exact-tie-breaker" was considered and rejected — remaining daily budget rarely
  lands on an exact tie across real accounts, so active_sessions would almost never actually
  influence anything, silently discarding the concurrency-spreading behavior that already
  works. Use:

      score = (remaining_daily_budget / daily_task_limit) - (active_sessions / max_concurrent)

  sorted descending. Both terms are normalized to a 0-1 range specifically so accounts on
  different plan tiers compare fairly — raw remaining-count comparison (e.g. 100 remaining on
  an Ultra account vs 13 remaining on a Free account) would always favor the higher-tier
  account by sheer magnitude even when it's proportionally more exhausted than the smaller
  one, which is the opposite of what this step is for.
- Get daily_task_limit and max_concurrent from whichever PLAN_LIMITS-based lookup
  has_capacity already performs internally — reuse that exact mechanism rather than
  introducing a new accessor. There is no limits attribute on the Account dataclass as
  established in Step 3's work; don't add one for this alone if the existing lookup already
  gets you the same values.
- This is a single change to the one .sort() call at the end of acquire() — the role filter
  (Step 3) and source filter both just narrow the eligible list before that same sort runs
  once, so no other location needs touching for this step's actual requirement.
- Optional, cheap, your call: since Step 3 already extends AccountPool.status() with role,
  consider also surfacing each account's computed remaining-budget fraction there — gives an
  operator a way to see why the pool routed where it did, from the dashboard, without needing
  to reason about the formula. Not required for this step to be complete.

CONSTRAINTS
- Don't change has_capacity's definition or the daily-reset logic — only the ordering used to
  pick among already-eligible accounts.
- Preserve existing behavior when all eligible accounts have equal remaining-budget fraction —
  should reduce to the current active_sessions-based ordering in that case (the formula
  already guarantees this: with equal budget fractions, ordering is determined entirely by
  the active_sessions term), not introduce nondeterminism.
- Not this step's concern, and don't try to solve it here: acquire()'s sort-then-mutate
  sequence isn't inherently atomic under concurrent callers — that's a race-safety question,
  not a routing-quality one, and belongs with Step 9's capacity/exhaustion handling if it
  needs addressing at all.

ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
- Given two same-tier eligible accounts with equal active_sessions but different
  daily_tasks_used, acquire() prefers the one with more remaining daily budget.
- Given an Ultra account with high raw remaining budget but proportionally more exhausted
  than a Free account with lower raw remaining budget, acquire() prefers the proportionally
  fresher Free account — confirms the normalization, not just the raw-count case.
- Existing tests covering the current least-active-sessions behavior still pass where
  daily_tasks_used is equal across candidates.
